# 09 — Spatial System

## Space Is The Product

Space is not empty.

Space communicates.

Space slows people down.

Space guides attention.

Space creates emotion before content appears.

MindRoute should feel less like navigating a website

and more like walking through an exhibition.

The visitor moves through space.

Not sections.

---

# Spatial Philosophy

Imagine every scene existing inside one infinite environment.

The camera simply travels through it.

Nothing is recreated.

Nothing resets.

Everything already exists.

The user only discovers it.

---

# The Infinite World

The landing has no beginning.

The landing has no end.

Every scene feels like another area of the same universe.

Photography continues.

Particles continue.

The network continues.

Darkness continues.

Light continues.

The world never disappears.

---

# Layered Space

Every scene is composed of multiple depths.

Depth 01

Atmosphere.

Depth 02

Very distant photography.

Depth 03

Network.

Depth 04

Typography.

Depth 05

Interactive interface.

Depth 06

Cursor interactions.

Every layer moves differently.

This difference creates depth.

---

# Foreground

Foreground belongs to interaction.

Buttons.

Cursor.

Small interface.

Labels.

Everything closest to the visitor.

Foreground is precise.

Sharp.

Stable.

---

# Midground

The emotional layer.

Photography.

Typography.

Travel memories.

Maps.

Connections.

This is where the narrative happens.

---

# Background

Background is never passive.

It breathes.

Particles drift.

Fog moves.

Light changes.

The network evolves.

Darkness shifts.

Very slowly.

Almost unconsciously.

---

# Distance

Distance should be perceived.

Not measured.

Objects further away should feel:

Less contrasted.

Slightly blurred.

Less saturated.

Slower.

Never simply smaller.

---

# Vertical Space

Scrolling is movement through space.

Not movement down a page.

The visitor should feel:

"I've entered another room."

Never:

"I reached another section."

---

# Horizontal Space

Horizontal movement is symbolic.

Use it carefully.

Horizontal motion communicates:

Choice.

Divergence.

Alternative futures.

Multiple journeys.

Identity paths.

Never use horizontal movement as decoration.

---

# Scale

Objects change emotional meaning through scale.

Huge photography

creates immersion.

Tiny labels

create intimacy.

Oversized typography

creates reflection.

Small interface

creates confidence.

Scale is emotional.

Never arbitrary.

---

# Compression & Expansion

Scenes should constantly alternate between:

Compression

↓

Expansion

Example:

A narrow paragraph.

↓

A full-screen landscape.

↓

A minimal sentence.

↓

An enormous destination.

↓

Tiny interface.

↓

Huge darkness.

The experience breathes.

---

# Visual Gravity

Everything has weight.

Photography is heavy.

Typography is medium.

Particles are light.

The network floats.

Buttons anchor interaction.

Weight should always feel balanced.

Never let the interface dominate the composition.

---

# Floating Elements

Nothing should feel perfectly attached.

Some elements gently float.

Photography subtly shifts.

Nodes drift.

Particles breathe.

The visitor should perceive life.

Not animation.

---

# Anchors

Every scene needs one spatial anchor.

A mountain.

A destination.

A portrait.

A glowing network.

A giant sentence.

The anchor stabilizes the scene.

Everything else orbits around it.

---

# Invisible Continuity

Whenever possible,

one object survives into the next scene.

Examples:

A photograph slowly fades into another.

The network remains.

A destination moves across sections.

The same light source continues.

The visitor should rarely perceive a hard cut.

---

# Perspective

Perspective should be subtle.

Never exaggerated.

Very small depth cues create realism.

Avoid dramatic 3D.

Avoid exaggerated parallax.

Luxury products trust restraint.

---

# Camera Behaviour

The camera is calm.

Slow.

Human.

Documentary.

Never gaming.

Never cinematic spectacle.

The camera observes.

It never performs.

---

# Scene Composition

Each scene should answer:

Where am I?

↓

What is the focus?

↓

Where am I moving?

↓

What remains?

The visitor should never feel lost.

Only curious.

---

# Spatial Rhythm

Alternate between:

Open.

Closed.

Bright.

Dark.

Wide.

Narrow.

Busy.

Silent.

This rhythm creates emotional pacing.

---

# Interaction With Space

The cursor should reveal space.

Hover should illuminate depth.

Scrolling should uncover hidden layers.

Motion should expose the world.

Never simply animate objects.

Reveal dimensions.

---

# Mobile Space

Mobile is not a compressed desktop.

It is a different camera.

Closer.

More intimate.

Photography fills more of the screen.

Typography becomes more personal.

Depth remains.

Only the framing changes.

---

# Forbidden

Never build:

Flat sections.

Card stacks.

Centered content blocks.

Perfect vertical symmetry.

Infinite scrolling lists.

Equal spacing everywhere.

Rigid grids dominating composition.

The visitor should never feel trapped inside a layout.

---

# Spatial References

Study:

James Turrell installations.

Tadao Ando architecture.

Olafur Eliasson exhibitions.

Apple keynote stages.

Aesop retail spaces.

Louis Kahn.

The feeling should be architectural.

Not digital.

---

# Final Principle

The visitor should never remember scrolling.

They should remember moving.

When the landing disappears

and only the journey remains,

the spatial system has succeeded.
