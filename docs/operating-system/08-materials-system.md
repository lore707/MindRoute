# 08 — Materials System

## Material Philosophy

Materials are not visual effects.

Materials are physical sensations.

Every surface should feel like it could almost be touched.

The visitor should never feel they are looking at a website.

They should feel they are looking through windows into another world.

Every material exists to reinforce atmosphere.

Never decoration.

---

# Material Hierarchy

The experience is built using only five materials.

Darkness.

Light.

Glass.

Lines.

Photography.

Nothing else.

The fewer materials we use,
the stronger the identity becomes.

---

# Darkness

Darkness is a material.

It has depth.

It has softness.

It absorbs light.

It contains atmosphere.

Dark areas should never be completely empty.

Tiny particles.

Very subtle gradients.

Soft fog.

Microscopic grain.

Invisible movement.

Darkness is alive.

---

# Glass

Glass is used sparingly.

It separates layers.

It never becomes the design.

Glass should feel:

Thin.

Elegant.

Almost invisible.

Soft internal reflections.

Very subtle blur.

Warm transparency.

Never frosted UI cards.

Never oversized glass panels.

Never Apple Vision Pro imitation.

Glass exists only when depth is needed.

---

# Light

Light behaves naturally.

Never as UI.

It diffuses.

It scatters.

It fades.

It wraps around objects.

Light should always have direction.

Never random glows.

Never decorative bloom.

Think architecture.

Not software.

---

# Photography

Photography is treated like physical material.

Images never sit inside rectangles.

They fade into darkness.

Edges disappear.

Photos emerge from shadow.

Sometimes they feel like memories.

Sometimes like dreams.

Sometimes like windows.

Never like content blocks.

---

# The Network

The network is made of light.

Not strokes.

Not SVG lines.

Light.

Connections should feel suspended in space.

Tiny luminosity.

Soft edges.

Very thin.

Almost disappearing.

The visitor should feel they exist inside the atmosphere.

Not above it.

---

# Lines

Lines are structural.

Never decorative.

Never outlines.

Never separators.

Lines represent:

Connections.

Memories.

Travel.

Identity.

Movement.

They should feel almost hand-drawn by light.

Never perfectly mechanical.

Never geometric.

---

# Grain

Grain is mandatory.

Not visible.

Perceptible.

Every background should contain an extremely subtle organic texture.

Digital perfection feels artificial.

Small imperfections create warmth.

---

# Fog

Fog creates depth.

Never drama.

Never cinematic clichés.

Very low opacity.

Very slow movement.

Fog should separate layers naturally.

Not hide them.

---

# Shadows

Shadows are soft.

Large.

Diffuse.

Almost invisible.

Never hard.

Never floating-card shadows.

Depth comes from atmosphere.

Not elevation.

---

# Reflections

Reflections are restrained.

Very soft.

Almost imperceptible.

Only visible while moving.

Never mirror-like.

Never glossy UI.

Reflections should suggest material.

Not show it.

---

# Blur

Blur represents distance.

Not style.

Use blur to create depth.

Not visual effects.

Foreground remains sharp.

Background breathes softly.

---

# Edges

Nothing ends abruptly.

Edges dissolve.

Images fade.

Light fades.

Typography fades.

Glass disappears into darkness.

Everything feels continuous.

---

# Surfaces

Every surface should feel layered.

Nothing is perfectly flat.

Every object contains:

Depth.

Light.

Texture.

Motion.

Atmosphere.

The visitor should almost perceive thickness.

---

# Buttons

Buttons are physical objects.

Not colored rectangles.

They respond with:

Tiny compression.

Subtle light shift.

Very small shadow change.

Almost imperceptible.

Luxury interactions are quiet.

---

# Cards

Avoid cards whenever possible.

If information needs separation,

use light,

space,

depth,

or photography.

Not rectangles.

Cards only exist when absolutely necessary.

---

# Borders

Avoid borders.

If separation is required,

use light.

Transparency.

Distance.

Shadow.

Never obvious strokes.

---

# Icons

Icons should be minimal.

Fine.

Elegant.

Consistent.

Never playful.

Never illustrative.

Never colorful.

They support.

They never lead.

---

# Materials + Motion

Materials should react to movement.

Glass catches light.

Particles drift.

Lines stretch.

Photography breathes.

Darkness changes.

Nothing remains completely static.

---

# Material Temperature

Everything should feel warm.

Not cold.

Warm darkness.

Warm whites.

Warm reflections.

Warm shadows.

Warm light.

Technology should disappear.

Humanity should remain.

---

# Forbidden

Avoid:

Heavy blur.

Huge glass cards.

Neumorphism.

Glassmorphism as style.

Thick borders.

Bright outlines.

Hard shadows.

Plastic gradients.

Chrome effects.

Metallic UI.

Artificial reflections.

Liquid glass effects.

Digital gimmicks.

If the visitor notices the material,

it is already too much.

---

# References

Study materials from:

Apple product pages.

Aesop stores.

Muji architecture.

Japanese concrete architecture.

Tadao Ando.

Nothing hardware.

High-end museums.

Luxury hotels.

Architectural visualization.

Observe how materials create emotion through restraint.

Never through excess.

---

# Final Principle

Materials should disappear.

The visitor should never think:

"This glass looks beautiful."

They should think:

"This place feels real."

The highest compliment is invisibility.

When materials stop being noticed,

they have succeeded.
